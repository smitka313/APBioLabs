Investigation 5

Photosynthesis

What factors affect the rate of photosynthesis in living leaves?

Unit 3: Cellular EnergeticsENE-1.IENE-1.JScience Practice 6.B≈ 3–4 class periods
    Overview

    What you'll investigate

    Photosynthesis is hard to watch directly, but a cheap trick makes it visible. Punch small disks from a leaf, pull the air out of them so they sink, and put them under a lamp in a bicarbonate solution. As the disks make oxygen, they float back up. How fast they rise tells you how fast net photosynthesis is going.

    First you will learn the floating leaf disk assay: infiltrating disks with a syringe, counting floating disks each minute, and summarizing a trial with the ET50, the estimated time for 50% of the disks to float. Then you will design your own experiment that tests light intensity, light color, CO2 supply, temperature, or a leaf feature.

    Learning objectives
    • ENE-1.I Describe the photosynthetic processes that allow organisms to capture and store energy.
    • ENE-1.J Explain how cells capture energy from light and transfer it to biological molecules for storage and use.
    Science practice
    • 6.B Support a claim with evidence from biological principles, concepts, processes, and/or data.

    You will also practice building indices (ET50), using reciprocals to replot data, and using medians and ±2 SE error bars to show variability.

    Safety in the real lab

    Wear goggles and an apron. You will work close to hot, exposed lightbulbs: keep solutions and wet hands away from bulbs and electrical cords, since a drip can shatter a hot bulb. Do not lean on lamps. Test your syringes before use, because some fail under vacuum. If you study temperature, never heat a solution above 50–60 °C.

    Background

    Measuring photosynthesis with buoyancy

    In photosynthesis, chloroplasts capture light energy and use it to make carbohydrate from CO2 and water, releasing O2:

    2 H2O + CO2 + light → (CH2O) + O2 + H2O

    In the light reactions, pigments in the thylakoid membranes absorb photons. The energy splits water, releasing O2, and drives the production of ATP and NADPH. In the Calvin cycle, the enzyme rubisco fixes CO2, and ATP and NADPH power its reduction to sugar. For every CO2 fixed, one O2 is released, so a mole of glucose (C6H12O6) means six moles of O2. You could measure either CO2 used or O2 made. This lab tracks O2.

    Why the disks sink and rise

    The spongy mesophyll is full of air spaces, so leaf disks normally float. If you hold a disk under vacuum in a syringe of solution, the gas is pulled out of those spaces. When the vacuum is released, liquid floods in. The disk is now denser than the solution and sinks. A drop of dilute soap acts as a wetting agent so the solution can get past the waxy leaf surface.

    The solution contains sodium bicarbonate (HCO3−), which gives the chloroplasts a supply of CO2. In the light, photosynthesis releases O2 into the air spaces. Bubbles build up until the disk becomes buoyant and rises. But leaf cells have mitochondria too. Cellular respiration uses some of that O2 at the same time. Rising disks therefore measure net photosynthesis (gross photosynthesis minus respiration). In the dark, respiration alone slowly uses up the trapped O2, and floating disks sink again.

    Turning floating disks into a rate

    Every minute you count how many disks are floating. Disks do not all rise together, and once in a while one never rises, so the median time is a better summary than the mean. The ET50 is the time when half the disks are floating. You can read it off the floating-versus-time graph by linear interpolation between the two minutes on either side of 50%.

    A faster process gives a smaller ET50, so graphing ET50 against light intensity slopes downward. Taking the reciprocal, 1/ET50 (min−1), gives a quantity that rises with the rate. Plotted against light, it makes the familiar curve that rises and then levels off.

    rate ∝ 1 / ET50     light intensity ∝ 1 / (distance)2

    Light intensity from a small lamp follows the inverse square law: doubling the distance spreads the light over four times the area, so only ¼ as many photons reach the disks. Chlorophyll a and b absorb blue and red light strongly and green light weakly, which is why leaves look green and why green light drives photosynthesis poorly.

    Before you start

    Procedure · Steps 1–6

    Infiltrate the leaf disks

    Set up. Make 300 mL of 0.2% sodium bicarbonate and pour about 3 cm into a cup labeled "With CO2". Fill a second cup with water only and label it "Without CO2". This cup is your control. Add one drop of dilute soap to each cup, and avoid making suds. Punch 10 uniform spinach disks for each cup, avoiding big veins.

    Infiltrate. Put 10 disks in each syringe, push the plunger in until less than 10% air remains, and draw up about 5 mL of the matching solution. Then cap the tip with a finger, pull back the plunger to make a vacuum for about 10 seconds while swirling, and release. This is the step that takes practice. Repeat until the disks sink, but more than three vacuums can damage them. If they still won't sink, add soap.

    Procedure · Steps 7–9

    Count floating disks and find ET50

    Put both cups under the lamp and start the timer. At the end of each minute, count the floating disks and then swirl the cups to free any disks stuck to the sides. Keep going until every disk in the bicarbonate cup is floating. To see respiration, put a box over the cups after the disks have floated and keep counting.

    Before you start: if you treat the disks in a way that increases net photosynthesis, should they float faster or slower? What do you predict for the water-only cup?

    Designing and conducting your investigation

    Test a variable of your choice

    Now that you can run the assay, choose a variable that might change the net rate of photosynthesis. It could be environmental (light intensity, color, CO2 supply, temperature) or a feature of the leaf (plant type, leaf age). Each cup holds 10 disks, and each cup gives one ET50. Use several cups per level so you can see how much results vary.
