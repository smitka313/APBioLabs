Why does growth slow?

A limiting factor is something that restricts a population’s growth or size. It could be a resource in short supply, such as food, or a condition such as extreme heat.

What this simulation models · sources and limits

Adapted from the OpenSciEd B.1 Lesson 5 Serengeti Kinesthetic Model. Two people in the guide’s example class are observers; the other 20 roles start as two wildebeest and 18 grass units. One available grass unit can feed one wildebeest. Unfed animals leave the herd; half of the fed animals, rounded down, contribute one new animal each. Roles switch between rounds, so the model is not a literal count of plants growing in the Serengeti.

The comparison changes the share of grass roles available to feed the herd, rounding down to whole units. This is a controlled test, not a real drought forecast. No predation, disease, migration, or rainfall is simulated. The rounding rule, fixed role pool, and reproduction rule are classroom simplifications; carrying capacity in the field can change when conditions change.

The habitat challenge models one immediate need on each piece to make competition visible. Real animals need several resources at once, and shortages can affect health or cause movement before they affect survival. Shelter is modeled as limited access to usable shade or cover, not dens or a universal driver of wildebeest numbers. The shortage trials change one resource on purpose; a real drought could reduce both forage and water. Invented stocks and a short run do not estimate real carrying capacity.
