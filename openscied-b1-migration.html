    Look for patterns in nutritional health.

    These cards are monthly group averages for sampled male wildebeest from several studies, not individual animals tracked over time. Bone-marrow fat indicates nutritional health. Anthrax percentages came from an outbreak year. The cards cannot tell you what fraction of the whole herd died or got sick.

    “Healthy” is the source’s nutritional category. The cards do not report a numerical cutoff for bone-marrow fat.

    Month and location are linked. The cards pair each month with one location. These records cannot separate the effect of month from the effect of location.

    Compare groups, not the fate of one animal. The “died from other causes” cards show 90% bone-marrow fat in May and 23% in August. Those are different sampled groups at different locations, not the same wildebeest losing fat. The cards do not report the sample size for either group. The OpenSciEd Teacher Edition (Lesson 3, p. 88) reports 126 carcasses sampled in 1968–1973 in one study, not the number behind any one card.

    What this explorer models · sources & limits

    The animated map is a schematic redraw of the monthly migration maps supplied in OpenSciEd B.1 Lessons 3–4, credited there to Giovanni Tombazzi’s Serengeti maps in cooperation with Hoopoe Adventure Tours. It shows broad ranges, not GPS tracks, park boundaries, animal counts, or exact dates. Real routes and timing vary.

    Weather, grass, individual animals, river ripples, and movement between monthly ranges are original illustrations, not new measurements. Grass appearance is not a measurement of nutritional quality. Hiding these decorations does not change the source data.

    The 18 health cards preserve the supplied Lesson 3 card values and nutritional labels. They are selected monthly group averages for male wildebeest, not 18 individual animals or a representative mortality census. They cannot be used to calculate the percentage of the population that dies or has a particular health condition. They are drawn from several studies, not one continuous tracking study. The anthrax data comes from an outbreak year.

    The rainfall image preserves the original Concord Consortium chart in Lesson 4 slide T (1961–1990 station averages). The three points are one classroom example year from Lesson 4 Data Excursion 2, Annual Rainfall and Wildebeest Occupancy. We have not generated or digitized a replacement rainfall dataset.

    Regional descriptions adapt the Lesson 4 Regional Data Cards. The explorer supports the lessons; it does not replace the scientist interviews, classroom discussions, or the full datasets in CODAP (the interactive data tool).
