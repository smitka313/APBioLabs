How to play

Start as carbon dioxide in the atmosphere. Roll once, read the station card and record. At Producers, a first roll of 1 or 2 records growth; your next roll uses the second table. Staying put counts as a turn. Make at least 10 rolls; your teacher decides when to stop. Rolls do not represent equal lengths of time.

What this activity models · sources and limits

Based on OpenSciEd B.2 Lesson 9, Teacher Edition pp. 188 and 194–196, and the student Carbon/Energy Quantitative Model handout. All carbon starts in atmospheric carbon dioxide. Seven locations, a six-sided die, and at least ten recorded rolls follow the handout. Card prose is preserved as supplied, including wording inconsistencies. Energy phrases such as “flows to another new system” are grouped under the handout’s transfer category for totals; the unchanged card text is always shown. Matter and energy categories use the handout’s summary table; “Biomass” matches that table (called “body mass” in its instructions).

Dice outcomes and event wording follow the supplied OpenSciEd Lesson 9 location cards. A Producers roll of 1 or 2 records growth and switches to that card’s second table for the next roll. Every roll, including this growth roll and stays, is recorded once. Counts describe this journey; they are not measured global carbon stocks, residence times or energy quantities. Carbon atoms are conserved; energy transfers between systems and can leave as heat.

Your carbon journey is complete

Follow a carbon atom

  1. Roll the die at your current station.
  2. Predict the destination, or choose to reveal the card.
  3. Read the official event. Inspect its matter and energy if you need more detail.
  4. Record & travel. Stays count as a turn too.

Complete at least ten rolls. A Producers roll of 1 or 2 records growth; the next roll uses the card’s second table. Rolls are not equal lengths of time. If a route repeats, the activity pauses for a reference card. Reference cards make sure you can see the stations your rolls missed; they do not move your atom or count as rolls.
