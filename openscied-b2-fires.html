Part A · Lesson 3 · 2 · First ideas

Why has the peat not rotted away?

Background

  • Zombie fires smolder in winter and flare up in spring.
  • Peat is layers of dead plants that never fully rotted. Permafrost is ground that stays frozen for years.
  • Decomposers such as bacteria and fungi break down dead plants. Some of the carbon returns to the air as carbon dioxide; some stays in decomposers or soil. Energy is transferred, including as heat.

Part B · Lesson 5 · 7 · Hypothesis

Could Earth’s tilt change how much energy plants store?

Background

  • Earth’s tilt shifts slowly over thousands of years. It changes how much sunlight reaches the Arctic.
  • Photosynthesis uses light to make sugar from carbon dioxide and water. Sugar stores matter and energy.
  • A directional hypothesis says what you change, what you measure and which way it will go.

Part B · Lesson 5 · 8 · Tilt Investigation

Run the tilt test

Background

  • Bromothymol blue (BTB) is a dye. It turns from blue toward yellow as carbon dioxide builds up.
  • Photosynthesis uses carbon dioxide in light. Respiration releases it day and night.
  • The more a lamp is tilted, the less light lands on the plant. At 70° about one third reaches it.

In the class example (direct light, 45°, and foil), as more light reached the plant, the BTB…
What this simulation models
  • Light on the plant is the cosine of the lamp’s angle: full light at 0°, about 0.71 at 45°, about 0.34 at 70°, none under foil.
  • For each tube, CO₂ change = plant size × (respiration per size − photosynthesis per size), in relative model units. Respiration per size is 0.30; photosynthesis per size is 1.10 × light ÷ (0.35 + light). Light is a fraction from 0 to 1 (for example, 71% is about 0.71). Negative means the plant removed more CO₂ than it released.
  • BTB starts at 2.0. For a CO₂ decrease, the model adds the signed change divided by 0.60 to 2.0. For a CO₂ increase, it adds 13 times the change. The model caps BTB at 1.0–6.0 and rounds to one decimal. Different CO₂ changes can give the same color name or even the same rounded BTB number.
  • Plant size varies a little, so tubes with the same setting differ a little. Real BTB is less tidy: its color depends on its starting pH and the water. The starch test shows leftover sugar stored as starch.
