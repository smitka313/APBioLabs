Cancer Rates Data Dive

Do the same patterns show up when you change what you count or who you compare? Make a prediction, sort real U.S. data, and save the evidence that supports your thinking.

01 · Your starting idea

Will the rankings match?

Real U.S. data

Imagine two lists of cancers: one ranked by new diagnoses, and one by deaths. Will the same cancer be at the top of both lists?

Two different measurements

Incidence counts new diagnoses. Mortality counts deaths. Each is shown as a rate per 100,000 in the population named by that source.

Why use a rate?

Rates make different-sized populations easier to compare. For example, 20 events out of 200,000 people equals 10 per 100,000. Age-adjusted rates take it a step further by using the same standard age mix.

We use group statistics. No one needs to share personal or family health information.

02 · Work with the data

Find a pattern worth saving

Does access change what gets detected?

Predict whether urban and higher-income communities will show more screening, more recorded diagnoses, both, or neither. Then compare counties and look for a counterexample. A diagnosis tells you a cancer was found and recorded, not when it began.

Check your hypothesis against national evidence

In a 2022 CDC survey, 77.2% of women ages 50–74 in metro areas reported a mammogram in the previous two years, compared with 73.1% outside metros. The estimates were 82.6% for households earning $75,000 or more and 63.1% below $15,000.

In CDC registry data for 2018–2022, female breast diagnoses per 100,000 were higher in metro counties. Lung and colorectal diagnoses showed the opposite pattern. These are different datasets, not the same people or proof of why the rates differ.

Investigate access: screening → follow-up testing → training
  1. FDA mammography facility finder: Can you find a screening site near each county you picked?
  2. ACR imaging facility finder: Search Michigan and look for breast ultrasound or other accredited imaging. Which follow-up services are listed? Check for confirmed accreditation, since this directory also includes applications in progress. An empty search is not proof a service is unavailable.
  3. CAAHEP program finder: Search Michigan for Diagnostic Medical Sonography. A program listing shows a training location, not the number of places available or how often working staff can practice.

Record what you find and what you still need to know. Distance, referral, appointment availability, cost, staff, equipment and supplies can all matter. Breast ultrasound can be used for follow-up or as a supplement, not as a replacement for routine mammography.

    Data sources and what the charts can’t tell us

    These data describe groups, not individual risk or genetics. A graph can suggest a question, but it does not prove what caused a difference. Age adjustment makes some comparisons fairer; it does not control for things such as screening, environmental exposure, or access to care.

    Counts and rates answer different questions. The people diagnosed in a year are not necessarily the same people who died that year. Dividing one published rate by the other does not tell you the chance of surviving.
