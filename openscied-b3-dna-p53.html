What this simulation models

Modeled: unzipping, pairing A with T and G with C, occasional wrong pairs, and three versions of p53.

Simplified: a real cell copies billions of letters, proofreads as it goes, and also gains or loses letters. In a real cell p53 works more like a manager: it senses damage and switches on genes that pause the cell cycle, call in repair proteins, or, if damage is too great, tell the cell to die. In the model only p53 (a) fits and works.

Short reading

DNA and chromosomes

Each chromosome is one long DNA molecule. A gene is a section of it that codes for one protein.

In S phase the cell copies its DNA. A wrong letter that stays is a mutation, so new cells’ chromosomes differ.

Show more

A human cell copies about 6 billion letters. Copying is very accurate, and cells fix many mistakes. Most mutations are harmless, and cancer usually takes several changes in genes that control cell division.

Check my checklist against the key ideas
  • Chromosomes are made of DNA (reading).
  • DNA is copied in S phase so each new cell gets a full set (Cell Game).
  • Unwind, unzip, then pair A with T and G with C (simulation).
  • A wrong letter is a substitution, a kind of mutation (simulation).
  • p53 (a) removes a mismatch from the finished copy; (b) and (c) do not fit (simulation).
  • Each new cell gets a copy (Cell Game).
  • An unfixed mutation is copied into the new cell, so its chromosomes differ. Most changes alone do not cause cancer.

Watch out: in the model, p53 fixes a mistake after copying. It does not stop mistakes.

    What this simulation models

    Modeled: copying DNA into RNA (U in place of T), cutting out parts of the copy, and reading groups of three to pick amino acids.

    Short reading

    DNA and p53

    The p53 gene is on chromosome 17. A different DNA code makes a different amino acid chain and protein shape, and shape decides how well p53 fits DNA.

    Show more

    The real p53 gene is roughly 18,000 to 20,000 base pairs; the model strands had 36 to 39, so the model is about 500 times shorter. The lesson says p53 has 375 amino acids; the usual full-length form has 393. The model had 8 or 9. Either way, real genes and proteins are much bigger. Not all DNA codes for a protein: some turns genes on or off.

    Glossary
    Amino acid
    A unit of a protein chain.
    Base
    A DNA letter: A, T, G or C (U in RNA).
    Complementary
    Fitting as a pair: A–T, G–C (A–U in RNA).
    Gene
    A section of DNA that codes for a protein.
    Mutation
    A change in DNA. A substitution swaps one letter.
    Protein
    A chain of amino acids that does work. Shape matters.
    Ribosome
    Reads RNA and builds proteins.
    RNA
    A copy of a gene’s code.
