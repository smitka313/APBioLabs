Look inside the ultrasound cart

The probe sends sound waves into the body. Echoes returning from tissues form a picture. In this model, ultrasound is used only for selected follow-up cases. An image alone does not tell you whether someone has cancer.

What this model leaves out

The same 30 fictional patients are used for every plan. Screening and follow-up visits have different costs. A visit is missed if it exceeds a patient’s available credits or time per visit. Patient burden includes fees, net travel cost, and time valued in credits. The clinic budget is separate. Equipment acquisition is a one-period allocation, not a real purchase price. An area’s modeled travel time does not tell you its wealth or cancer risk. Facilities with more resources may be better able to pay for equipment and trained staff, but funding, staffing and geography do not always line up.

Real screening and follow-up depend on the cancer type, the person and clinical guidance. The model does not simulate how accurate any test is, cancer incidence, or confirmed diagnoses. See National Cancer Institute: How Cancer Is Diagnosed.
