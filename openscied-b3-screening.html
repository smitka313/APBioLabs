Your mission: Stay within the 720-credit clinic budget, get all three featured patients to needed care, and help more people district-wide than the hospital plan did. Open a cost sheet to practice booking a visit.

All incomes and distances are fictional. This model sets aside 2% of monthly income for test fees, travel, and time away. Distances are one way.

How the model calculates costs

Screening costs 8 credits; ultrasound costs 22. Driving costs 1 credit per 2 one-way miles, plus a 3-credit detour for some cases. A voucher subtracts up to 13 credits from the drive, never below zero, and saves no time.

Trip and appointment time is 1 hour plus 1 hour per 14 one-way miles, plus 1 hour for a detour where shown. Time away is counted at each patient’s listed rate (4–7 credits per hour). This is not a bill; it represents the cost of lost time.

    What this model leaves out

    The same 30 fictional patients are used for every plan. Screening and follow-up visits have different costs. A visit is missed if it exceeds a patient’s available credits or time per visit. Patient burden includes fees, net travel cost, and time valued in credits. The clinic budget is separate. Equipment acquisition is a one-period allocation, not a real purchase price. Each visit starts from the patient’s home. An area’s modeled travel time does not tell you its wealth or cancer risk. Facilities with more resources may be better able to pay for equipment and trained staff, but funding, staffing and geography do not always line up.

    Real screening and follow-up depend on the cancer type, the person and clinical guidance. The model does not simulate how accurate any test is, cancer incidence, or confirmed diagnoses. See National Cancer Institute: How Cancer Is Diagnosed.
