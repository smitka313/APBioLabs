01 · Starting idea

Make a prediction

Meet the starting colony

Every rat has two copies of the gene we’re tracking. Four rats start the model: one RB and three BB.

A rat inherits one copy of this gene from each parent. R and B are two versions of the same gene, called alleles.

R is the red resistance allele; B is the blue allele. RR means two R copies, RB means one of each, and BB means two B copies. In this model, one R is enough to show the resistant trait.

After the selection pressure is introduced, how do you think the percentage of rats with the resistant trait will change?

03 · Read the data

Three ways to describe a colony

Plot your trial

Survivors and starting counts are separate snapshots. A trait percentage can go up even when the colony gets smaller. Pairings vary by trial, but the model uses expected offspring counts rather than chance inheritance in each pup. Real colonies can change by chance as well as by selection.

Your generation record

How are the measures calculated?
Population size
RR rats + RB rats + BB rats.
Percent showing resistance
Rats with at least one R ÷ all rats.
R allele frequency
Number of R copies ÷ all copies of this gene. RR contributes two R copies; RB contributes one.

What the model does and does not show

This is a classroom simulation, not a pest-control investigation. It models two alleles, one simplified trait, fixed expected offspring counts, and nonoverlapping generations. During the selection steps, RR and RB survive and BB does not. Rats do not acquire R because they need it; offspring can inherit it from parents. No rat population measurements come from this simulation.
