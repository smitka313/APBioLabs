Cell Division: Mitosis and Meiosis

How do eukaryotic cells divide to produce genetically identical cells or to produce gametes with half the normal DNA?

Unit 4: Cell CycleUnit 5: HeredityIST-1.B, 1.C, 1.F, 1.GPractices 1.B, 4.B, 5.A≈ 4 lab periods + discussion
    At a glance

    What students do and why

    The manual's investigation has five parts: modeling mitosis, scoring onion root tips treated with a lectin, comparing normal and cancer karyotypes, modeling meiosis, and measuring crossover frequency in Sordaria fimicola. The interactive page covers all five, plus a student-designed root-tip experiment. The manual budgets at least four 45-minute lab periods plus a discussion of cell cycle control; with the simulations, Parts 1 and 4 fit in one period, Parts 2 and 5 in about one period each, and Part 3 in half a period.

    Preparation

    Real materials, summarized

    Parts 1 and 4 (models): any reusable chromosome model works. Options in the manual are sockosomes (stuffed children's socks with Velcro centromeres, three pairs per group of four), modeling clay with twist ties, pipe-cleaner pieces with beads, or pop-it beads. Mark alleles with tape or marker so crossing over is visible.

    Part 2 (root tips): onion sets or scallions, two jars with fine sand (control wet with water; treatment wet with 50–75 mL lectin solution, PHA-M 10 mg in 200 mL water, weighed in a fume hood with gloves). Keep the jars in the dark 1.5–2 days, harvest roots, fix 4–18 h in Carnoy's (125 mL glacial acetic acid + 375 mL 95% ethanol), rinse, and store in 70% ethanol at 4 °C for up to several weeks. Do this a week ahead. Students then soften tips in 1 M HCl (4 min), return them to Carnoy's (4 min), keep the distal 2 mm, stain with carbol-fuchsin (2 min), blot, add water, tease apart, and squash with a pencil eraser through a lab wipe. Treating the bulbs yourself lets you label jars A and B so students score blind.

    Part 3: karyotype images of normal and HeLa cells, and a CML karyotype with a Philadelphia chromosome. The page supplies drawn versions.

    Part 5: a purchased Sordaria wild type × tan cross plate (one per 4–6 students), slides, cover slips, toothpicks, and lab wipes. Scrape perithecia from where the two strains meet.

    Safety

    Goggles and gloves for HCl, Carnoy's, carbol-fuchsin (contains phenol), and razor blades. Purified lectin is an irritant. Students press cover slips with an eraser, never a thumb.

    How the simulations map to the bench

    Models, numbers, and limits

    Part 1 · Mitosis model

    Students replicate DNA, condense, then must drag each duplicated chromosome to the plate and drag each chromatid to a pole. Buttons can do the alignment and pull for students who cannot drag. If both sisters go to one pole, the daughters are reported as aneuploid, which answers the manual's "what if sister chromatids fail to separate?" question. Six drawn onion cells must be swapped into order. Limit: the model omits prometaphase and the spindle checkpoint itself.

    Part 2 · Root tips

    Each field is a generated squash of ~50–60 whole cells. A tip's mitotic index is drawn from a normal distribution (SD 0.025) around 0.145 for control and 0.353 for lectin, matching the manual's sample (25/173 and 88/249). Mitotic cells are split 45% prophase, 20% metaphase, 12% anaphase, 23% telophase, roughly in proportion to phase duration. Unchecking procedure steps produces the classic failures: no HCl or no squash gives overlapping, out-of-focus layers; skipping the 2 mm cut gives elongation-zone cells with almost no mitosis; no stain gives a nearly blank field. Students' own classifications, not the hidden key, go into Table 1, so misidentifications become real error. "Check my IDs" compares with the key; "Auto-score" is a time-saver for demos. Seven other groups are simulated for the class data, or you can load the manual's sample totals.

    The χ² panel offers both of the manual's methods: the student handout's goodness-of-fit (expected from control proportions, applied to treated cells) and the teacher pages' 2 × 2 contingency (Tables 2–4). Both use df = 1 and critical value 3.84. Students must type expected values before the check button marks them.

    Part 3 · Karyotypes

    Chromosomes are drawn to scale from approximate human chromosome lengths (GRCh38) with centromere positions and generated banding that matches between homologs. The normal cell is 46,XX. The CML cell is 46,XY,t(9;22)(q34;q11): one chromosome 9 is lengthened and one 22 is the short Philadelphia chromosome. The HeLa cell is a representative composite, not a specific published karyotype: hypertriploid with 82 chromosomes (the ATCC-reported modal number), mostly three or four copies of each, plus nine illustrative marker chromosomes (for example an isochromosome 5p, which is a known recurrent HeLa marker). Real HeLa spreads range from about 70 to more than 160 chromosomes.

    Part 4 · Meiosis model

    Gene A sits four beads below the long chromosome's centromere. A chiasma at beads 1–4 swaps A/a between non-sister chromatids; a chiasma at bead 5 lies beyond the gene and leaves it unrecombined, which illustrates why crossover frequency rises with gene–centromere distance. Tetrads can be flipped at metaphase I. Nondisjunction options: the long pair in meiosis I, or the long chromatids in one meiosis II cell. The 200-meiosis run shows a 1:1:1:1 gamete ratio for genes on different chromosomes, tested with χ² (df = 3).

    Part 5 · Sordaria

    Each field is a squashed perithecium with a rosette of 20–23 asci plus a few loose ones. A crossover between the tan gene and the centromere occurs in 52% of meioses, giving the published 26 map units. Crossover asci show 2:2:2:2 or 2:4:2 (either orientation, equally likely); about 14% of asci are immature and cannot be scored, and asci running off the field are refused. Students record each field, then compute % crossover and map units themselves. Expect individual estimates of roughly 20–33 map units from ~60 asci (binomial SD ≈ 3 map units). Limit: real squashes include overlapping and ambiguous asci and occasional aberrant patterns that the simulation omits.

    Design your own

    Factor responses are illustrative assumptions, chosen to be qualitatively reasonable: lectin raises MI with saturation (MI = 0.145 + 0.287·c/(c + 20), c in µg/mL); NaCl lowers it (MI = 0.145/(1 + c/90)); pH has an optimum near 6.4; temperature an optimum near 24 °C; root-knot nematodes raise it modestly. Tip-to-tip SD is 15% of MI + 0.012, and counts are binomial. Tell students these are models, not published dose-response data.

    Common misconceptions

    What to listen for

    • "A G2 cell has 92 chromosomes." It has 46 chromosomes made of 92 chromatids. Count centromeres. Duplicated DNA adds no new information.
    • Counting chromatids after separation. At anaphase each separated chromatid is a chromosome, so a human cell briefly has 92.
    • Homologs pair in mitosis. They do not; students who drag pairs side by side onto the plate are modeling meiosis I.
    • Memorized stage names without process. The manual warns of this; ask what is moving and why at each step.
    • A higher mitotic index means faster division. Not necessarily: a cell arrested in mitosis (for example by colchicine) raises MI while slowing division. MI is a snapshot of a fraction, not a rate.
    • Crossing over happens between sister chromatids. The meaningful exchanges are between non-sister chromatids of homologs.
    • Map units = % crossover asci. Only half the spores in a crossover ascus are recombinant, so divide by 2.
    • "Cancer cells just divide faster." The core issue is loss of control: ignoring checkpoints, growth signals, and apoptosis, with genome instability as a result.
    • The Philadelphia chromosome is from crossing over. It is a reciprocal translocation between non-homologous chromosomes 9 and 22, not an exchange between homologs.

    Pre-lab quiz

    3. M-spindle (metaphase) checkpoint.
    4. Cyclin is degraded; CDK without cyclin is inactive.
    5. Lectin has no effect on the proportion of cells in mitosis.

    Getting Started: asexual reproduction lets a well-adapted plant spread quickly without a mate and keeps successful genotypes intact. Defective control leads to uncontrolled division, accumulation of mutations, and possibly cancer.

    Part 2 expected results

    Simulated class data typically give control MI ≈ 13–16% and lectin MI ≈ 32–38%, with χ² far above 3.84. The manual's sample totals give:

    The manual reports 22.06 because it rounds expected values to whole numbers, and its Sample Table 3 lists 179 for treated interphase where its Table 4 correctly uses 182. With the student handout's goodness-of-fit method, expected treated values are 213.0 (interphase) and 36.0 (mitosis), giving χ² ≈ 87.9. Either way, reject the null hypothesis.

    Why collect class data?
    Larger samples reduce the effect of tip-to-tip variation and misclassification, and give enough cells for χ² expected values to be reliable.
    Did the lectin increase mitosis?
    The data support a higher proportion of cells in mitosis in treated roots. That is consistent with lectin acting as a mitogen, though it does not prove the soybean pathogen's lectin did so in the field.
    Further experiments
    Blind scoring, several lectin concentrations, a heat-denatured lectin control, other species (soybean itself), measuring root length and tissue strength.
    Does more cells in mitosis mean faster division?
    Not necessarily. MI is the fraction in mitosis at fixation; it rises if mitosis lengthens or cells arrest, as well as if cycling speeds up.
    Other ways to measure rate
    Time-course sampling, labeling S-phase cells (BrdU/EdU) and following them, root elongation rate, or cell counts over time.

    Part 3

    Normal: 46 chromosomes in matched pairs. HeLa: 82 in this composite, with three or four copies of most chromosomes and rearranged markers; aneuploidy and structural instability reflect broken checkpoints. HPV-18 E6 targets p53 for degradation and E7 inactivates Rb, so damaged cells pass G1/S without arrest or apoptosis. CML: 46 chromosomes, but one 22 is shortened (Philadelphia) and one 9 is lengthened; the t(9;22) fuses BCR to ABL1, making a constantly active tyrosine kinase that drives division (target of imatinib). Normal cells monitor DNA through damage sensors that activate p53, which halts the cycle for repair or triggers apoptosis.

    Part 4

    DNA replicates once, before meiosis I. Homologs carry the same genes but can have different alleles. Crossing over is reciprocal exchange between non-sister chromatids at chiasmata; longer distances leave more room for a crossover, and interference and centromere proximity reduce it. Independent assortment: 2n combinations (223 = 8,388,608 in humans). Nondisjunction in meiosis I makes all four gametes abnormal; in meiosis II, two of four. Trisomy 21 usually arises from maternal meiosis I nondisjunction. Cri du chat is a deletion of 5p, a structural change rather than a number change. Mitosis keeps chromosome number and produces identical cells; meiosis halves it and produces variation.

    Part 5 · Evaluating results

    1. Only half of the spores in a crossover ascus are recombinant, and map units measure recombinant products.
    2. Class values should cluster near 26 map units (often 22–30).
    3. Small samples, misscoring overlapping or immature asci, counting the same ascus twice, and multiple crossovers that restore a 4:4 pattern (which underestimates distance); environmental conditions on the plate may also matter.
    4. Diagrams should show a crossover between the gene and the centromere, then meiosis II spindle orientations producing 2:2:2:2 or 2:4:2, then one mitosis doubling each nucleus.
    5. The Philadelphia chromosome is a translocation between non-homologous chromosomes, not the homologous crossing over seen in Sordaria.
    6. Largely yes: cyclin-CDK control and spindle checkpoints also operate in meiosis, but meiosis skips S phase between divisions and adds controls for synapsis and crossing over.
    Student-designed investigations

    Ideas that work

    • Lectin concentration series to find a dose-response curve and saturation.
    • Salinity (NaCl), pH, or temperature of the sand water, as the manual suggests.
    • A biotic factor such as root-knot nematodes or a fungal culture filtrate.
    • Distance from the root cap: compare cells within the distal 2 mm with cells farther back (the manual's alternative procedure).
    • Caffeine or colchicine (teacher-handled) to show that MI can rise even when division is blocked.
    • Sordaria crossover frequency at different incubation temperatures or humidity.

    Expect students to state null and alternative hypotheses, include a control level, use at least three tips per level, show ±2 SE error bars, and run χ² against the control.

    Extensions

    Where to go next

    • Have students act out chromosome movement or make a short video of the bead model.
    • Case study "But I'm Too Young!" (National Center for Case Study Teaching in Science) on cell cycle control and ovarian cancer.
    • The Immortal Life of Henrietta Lacks (Rebecca Skloot): consent, compensation, patents on tissues, and bias in care. A structured debate works well.
    • Tumor suppressors and proto-oncogenes: Rb–E2F at G1/S, p53, BRCA1/2, and c-sis (PDGF β-chain) carried by simian sarcoma virus.
    • Ask how much variation there would be without crossing over, and list every mechanism of variation seen in the lab: crossing over, independent assortment, segregation, nondisjunction, random fertilization, and mutation.
